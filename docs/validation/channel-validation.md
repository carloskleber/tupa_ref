# Lightning channel in air — validation

ROADMAP Phase 10b item 3 ([ADR 0025](../adr/0025-lightning-channel-and-two-node-sources.md),
theory.md §4.5). Everything below is regenerated by
[`channel_checks.py`](channel_checks.py) (`--fortran-bin PATH [--only chen|table3|zc|kappa]`; needs
numpy and the Fortran executable). The reference is Baba & Rakov, *Electromagnetic
Models of the Lightning Return Stroke* [44], and the chapters on antenna-theory
return-stroke models in Cooray (ed.), *Lightning Electromagnetics* [74].

All cases use `imageModel: "ideal"` (a perfectly conducting ground plane, the
reference's setup) and the NLT transient path; numbers are quoted over the part
of the record before the top reflection.

## 1. Unloaded channel against Chen's analytic current

A vertical perfectly conducting cylinder (2 km, r₀ = 0.23 m, 10 m segments) above
perfect ground, driven at the base by a voltage ramp (5 MV, 1 µs rise) —
`common/channel_unloaded.json`. Chen's closed form for the step response of a
monopole, `I = 2·(2V/η)·atan(π / (2 ln(√(c²t² − z²)/a)))` for `ct > z` (twice
the dipole value for the monopole over ground), is convolved with the ramp.

| height z (m) | window (µs) | worst \|I_TUPÃ − I_Chen\| / peak (%) | peak TUPÃ / Chen (kA) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 5 | 2.0–12.0 | 1.32 | 13.56 / 13.29 |
| 305 | 3.0–11.8 | 1.45 | 11.32 / 11.48 |
| 605 | 4.0–10.8 | 1.34 | 10.78 / 11.04 |
| 905 | 5.0–9.8 | 1.50 | 10.50 / 10.78 |

The windows start 2 µs after the front's arrival (the front itself is where a
10 m segmentation and the finite 5 MHz band disagree most with the closed form).
The peaks agree within 2.5 %. The Fortran test `test_channel` checks the same
comparison with a tighter geometry (100 × 10 m, window 3.0–4.5 µs, 1.5 %), and
the Rust `physics` test mirrors it.

## 2. Loaded wire speed against Table 3 of Baba & Rakov

A 0.23 m wire loaded with a uniform series inductance (`inductance` set
explicitly, no calibration), excited by a current ramp at the base. The speed is
measured between z ≈ 5 m and z ≈ 305 m with the same metric the calibration uses
(10–90 % tangent onset). The published values come from their FDTD runs.

| L′ (µH/m) | published (FDTD) | TUPÃ, 10 m segments | TUPÃ, 5 m segments | closed form `1/√(1+L′/L₀)`, z = 150 m |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 2 | 0.60c | 0.580c | 0.574c | 0.646c |
| 4 | 0.48c | 0.454c | 0.458c | 0.514c |
| 8 | 0.37c | 0.350c | 0.358c | 0.390c |

TUPÃ is 0.02–0.03c below the FDTD values on every row at 10 m, and the closed
form (which ignores the wire's finite radius and end effects) is 0.03–0.05c
above them, so the loading that gives a prescribed speed differs from the closed
form's by a few percent to tens of percent (§4): the closed form is the initial
value and the calibration the final word. Refining the segmentation from 10 m
to 5 m moves the speed by at most 0.008c.

## 3. Base impedance of the calibrated channel

`common/channel_loaded.json`: 3 km, r₀ = 0.03 m, target c/2, R′ = 0.5 Ω/m,
graded 5–20 m segments, calibrated (κ = 0.986, measured speed 1.4988·10⁸ m/s).
The current-ramp source gives `V/I` at the base (from the fixture
`channel_loaded_expected.csv`), compared with the transmission-line estimate
`(c/v)·60·ln(2vt/r₀)` of a channel growing at speed v:

| t (µs) | V/I at the base (Ω) | TL estimate (Ω) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1.5 | 1017 | 1153 |
| 2.0 | 1085 | 1188 |
| 3.0 | 1180 | 1236 |
| 4.0 | 1252 | 1271 |
| 5.0 | 1314 | 1297 |

The difference is −12 % at 1.5 µs and within 5 % from 3 µs on (+1 % at 5 µs). The
estimate ignores the finite rise time and R′, both of which matter early; the
level (1–1.3 kΩ) sits inside the 0.6–2.5 kΩ range the literature gives for
equivalent channel impedances [74].

## 4. Calibration scale

`calibrate: true` finds κ in `L′(z) = κ·L₀(z)(c²/v² − 1)` such that the measured
speed equals the target. 3 km channel, R′ = 0.5 Ω/m (the calibration channel is
lossless, so κ does not depend on R′). The last column is `L′` at the first and
last segment, in µH/m.

| r₀ (m) | target speed | segmentation | κ | measured speed | L′ first / last (µH/m) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0.03 | 0.67c | graded 5–20 m | 0.822 | 0.6711c | 1.03 / 2.46 |
| 0.03 | 0.67c | uniform 20 m | 0.847 | 0.6692c | 1.35 / 2.54 |
| 0.03 | 0.50c | graded 5–20 m | 0.986 | 0.4996c | 3.03 / 7.22 |
| 0.03 | 0.50c | uniform 20 m | 0.986 | 0.4995c | 3.85 / 7.22 |
| 0.03 | 0.33c | graded 5–20 m | 1.052 | 0.3303c | 8.81 / 21.02 |
| 0.03 | 0.33c | uniform 20 m | 1.054 | 0.3303c | 11.22 / 21.05 |
| 0.23 | 0.67c | graded 5–20 m | 0.887 | 0.6723c | 0.67 / 2.21 |
| 0.23 | 0.50c | graded 5–20 m | 1.012 | 0.5008c | 1.87 / 6.17 |
| 0.23 | 0.67c, 0.50c | uniform 20 m | refused | | |
| 0.23 | 0.33c | graded or uniform | refused | | |

κ lies between 0.82 and 1.05 on every accepted case, so the closed form is a good
initial value (within 18 %) and the calibration is a trim, and the segmentation
matters little for the thin channel. The cases refused are the thick ones with
coarse segments: with r₀ = 0.23 m and 20 m segments (or, for the slowest target,
also the graded 5–20 m set) the measured speed is not a monotonic function of κ (jitter
of ±10 %, with κ = 1 giving 0.39c for a 0.5c target), and the secant iteration
stays far from the target. The calibration refuses them with an error rather than
accepting a locked-on κ — one run had reached κ ≈ 29 at the target speed, which
is why a final κ outside 0.25–4 is an error. Use shorter segments for a thick
channel (the graded 5–20 m set works down to c/2).

## 5. Not done

The reduced-scale induced-voltage experiment of Ishii et al., as reproduced by
Pokharel et al. (a 25 m wire over 0.06 S/m ground, channel at 6 µH/m and
0.5 Ω/m, v ≈ 0.43c [74]), needs the measured waveform of the original
experiment, which exists in this repository only as a description. Every
segment of that geometry is in air, so the present model covers it once the
reference curve is digitised.
